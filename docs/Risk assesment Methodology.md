# Risk Assessment Methodology

## Purpose

## Likelihood Scale
| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rare: Very unlikely to occur |
| 2 | Unlikely: Could occur, but not expected |
| 3 | Possible: Could occur occasionally |
| 4 | Likely: Expected to occur at some point |
| 5 | Almost certain: Expected to occur frequently or soon |

## Impact Scale
| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Insignificant: Minimal effect; easily resolved |
| 2 | Minor: Limited effect on a small number of people or processes |
| 3 | Moderate: Noticeable operational, financial, privacy, or reputational effect |
| 4 | Major: Significant disruption, data exposure, financial loss, or legal concern |
| 5 | Severe: Extensive data breach, major outage, serious legal consequences, or severe reputational damage |

## Risk Rating

Risk rating is calculated as:

**Risk Rating = Likelihood × Impact**

| Score | Rating | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Monitor and manage through routine controls |
| 5–9 | Medium | Create a treatment plan and review regularly |
| 10–16 | High | Treat promptly and report to management |
| 17–25 | Critical | Immediate action required; escalation to senior management |

## Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance means formally deciding that the remaining risk is acceptable after considering the possible impact, cost of treatment, and business requirements.

- **Low risk:** May be accepted by the risk owner.
- **Medium risk:** Requires approval from the relevant department manager.
- **High risk:** Requires approval from senior management.
- **Critical risk:** Normally should not be accepted without immediate treatment or documented exceptional approval.

Every accepted risk must record the risk owner, approval authority, acceptance date, reason, treatment decision, and review date.
