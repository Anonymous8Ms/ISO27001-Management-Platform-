# Context and Interested Parties

## Internal Issues

### 1. Different levels of security awareness

**Issue:** Ikigai employees and academic staff may have different levels of information-security awareness, especially when handling student information, passwords, and suspicious emails.

**Effect on information security:** This may lead to phishing attacks, accidental disclosure of student data, weak password practices, or incorrect sharing of confidential information.

**Possible response:** Ikigai can provide security-awareness training, phishing-awareness exercises, and periodic reminders about handling student and company information.

### 2. Rapid changes to web and mobile applications

**Issue:** Ikigai may frequently introduce new features to its website and mobile applications, such as live classes, student dashboards, payment-related functions, or integrations with external services. These changes may not always receive a consistent security and privacy review before release.

**Effect on information security:** An inadequately reviewed feature could expose student personal information, create broken access controls, allow SQL injection or insecure API access, or send data to an unintended system.

**Possible response:** Ikigai should include security and privacy checks in its software-development and change-management process before a feature is released.

### 3. Unclear security ownership

**Issue:** Responsibilities for information security, privacy, incident response, vendor security, and regulatory communication may not be clearly assigned to specific people or teams.

**Effect on information security:** During a security incident, delayed decisions or duplicated work may increase the chance of data loss, prolonged system exposure, and missed reporting obligations.

**Possible response:** Ikigai should define security responsibilities, assign control owners, maintain an escalation process, and document who approves important security decisions.

### 4. Dependence on third-party technology vendors

**Issue:** Ikigai depends on external vendors such as cloud-service providers, payment-service providers, video-conferencing platforms, email providers, analytics services, and communication tools. These vendors may process, store, or transmit Ikigai and student-related information.

**Effect on information security:** A vendor security incident, service outage, weak security configuration, or unauthorized access could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of Ikigai’s information and services.

**Possible response:** Before onboarding a vendor, Ikigai should perform security and privacy due diligence. The contract should define security responsibilities, data-handling requirements, incident-notification timelines, audit or evidence rights, and service-exit requirements. Ikigai should periodically review important vendors and track unresolved vendor risks.


## External Issues

### 1. Changes in privacy and cybersecurity regulations

**Issue:** Ikigai may be affected by changes to privacy and cybersecurity laws, regulations, and regulatory guidance in the countries or regions where it operates or serves students and customers.

**Effect on information security:** A change in legal requirements may require Ikigai to modify how it collects, stores, shares, retains, and protects personal information. Failure to identify and respond to these changes could lead to non-compliance, complaints, penalties, or loss of customer trust.

**Possible response:** Ikigai should monitor relevant legal and regulatory developments and assess whether changes are needed in its policies, controls, contracts, systems, and privacy processes.

### 2. Changing cyber-threat landscape

**Issue:** Ikigai may face changing cyber threats such as phishing, ransomware, account takeover, denial-of-service attacks, software vulnerabilities, and attacks against cloud or mobile applications.

**Effect on information security:** New or changing threats may exploit weaknesses in Ikigai’s applications, accounts, infrastructure, or third-party services, which could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of student information and learning services.

**Possible response:** Ikigai should monitor relevant threats, review its risks periodically, apply security updates, test important controls, and update its incident-response and business-continuity plans.

### 3. Rapid development of artificial intelligence

**Issue:** New AI models and AI-enabled technologies are developing rapidly. If Ikigai introduces AI features such as an AI tutor, chatbot, or automated evaluation system, the organization may face new security, privacy, and safety risks.

**Effect on information security:** An AI feature may disclose personal information, reveal confidential course content, be manipulated through prompt injection, produce unsafe outputs, or use student data in an unintended way.

**Possible response:** Before introducing an AI feature, Ikigai should perform security, privacy, and safety assessments, define acceptable use, restrict the data available to the AI system, test for misuse, and monitor the feature after release.

### 4. Cloud and service-provider availability

**Issue:** Ikigai depends on external cloud and technology service providers to operate its learning platform, payment processing, live classes, email communication, and other services.

**Effect on information security:** A provider outage, security incident, data loss, or service disruption could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of Ikigai’s systems and student information.

**Possible response:** Ikigai should assess important providers, define security and availability requirements in contracts, monitor service performance, maintain backups, and prepare alternative procedures for critical services.



## Interested Parties and Their Requirements :-

### 1. Students and parents

**Requirement:** Their personal information, learning records, examination results, account credentials, and payment-related information should be collected, used, stored, and shared securely. They should receive clear information about how their data is used and have a way to raise complaints or privacy concerns.

**Classification:** Expectation and regulatory requirement.

### 2. Employees and contractors

**Requirement:** Employees and contractors should receive security training, access only the information required for their work, and follow Ikigai’s security policies and procedures. Ikigai must protect confidential information from unauthorized sharing by current or former employees by using confidentiality agreements, least-privilege access, access reviews, and timely access removal during offboarding.

**Classification:** Contractual and expectation.

 ### 3. Regulators and government authorities

**Requirement:** Ikigai should comply with applicable privacy, cybersecurity, child-data, consumer-protection, and breach-reporting requirements. It should maintain accurate records and cooperate with lawful requests, investigations, and notifications.

**Classification:** Regulatory.
